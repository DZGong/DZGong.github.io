VMAP: Voltage mapping by activated photocycle

Conventional voltage imaging only probes voltage fluctuations and cannot measure the absolute value of membrane potentials. VMAP achieves absolute voltage imaging by using a brief illumination pulse to probe the voltage-insensitive fluorescence (Fᵢ) as an internal reference to normalize the voltage measurement signal Fᵥ. The normalized signal Rᵥ = Fᵥ/Fᵢ serves as a robust metric for absolute membrane potential values with ~7 mV precision.

Absolute voltage, absolutely simple

Without relying on any specialized hardware or multi-labeling, the only requirement for performing VMAP is to turn light on and off at the sub-millisecond scale. This means VMAP can be readily integrated into regular voltage imaging systems and can flexibly accommodate other imaging modalities, such as optogenetics or calcium imaging.

Figure: VMAP traces of neurons under optogenetic stimulation.

See voltage at scale

Compared to electrode-based electrophysiology methods, the optical imaging nature of VMAP makes it able to perform voltage measurements over large areas, for long times, and non-invasively; thus, the throughput can be expanded by orders of magnitude.

Figure: VMAP of hiPSC colonies across millimeter-scale areas, showing distinct voltage spatial domains that match pluripotency markers.

MAP the uncharted

VMAP has been demonstrated across rodent, human, and fish cells; across isolated cells, cell assemblies, and intact animals; and across timescales from millisecond neuronal activity to days-long changes in resting potential. The ability to map absolute voltage across scales enables studies of bioelectrical activity that were previously inaccessible.
